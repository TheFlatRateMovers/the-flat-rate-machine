## Scope — what junk removal covers (industry consensus)

Accepted, non-hazardous: furniture of all kinds; mattresses and box springs;
appliances large and small; electronics / e-waste; exercise equipment; hot tubs;
yard debris (brush, branches, fencing, playsets); renovation and construction
debris (drywall, lumber, plywood, flooring, carpet, cabinets, countertops,
fixtures, roofing in load limits); office furniture and contents; bagged
household junk; whole-property cleanouts (estate, eviction, foreclosure,
garage / basement / attic).
Sources: The Junkluggers accepted-items guide (junkluggers.com); Full House Junk
Removal "What Can Junk Haulers Take"; Clutter Ninjas accepted / not-accepted guide.

## The boundary — never hauled (hazardous / regulated)

Wet paint and solvents; motor oil, fuel, antifreeze; pesticides and household
chemicals; pressurized containers (propane tanks, oxygen, helium); asbestos and
asbestos-containing material (licensed abatement trade); medical and biohazard
waste; lead paint materials. Gray / special-handling: tires, lead-acid batteries,
fluorescent tubes, CRT electronics, appliances containing refrigerant.
Sources: Junkluggers Nashville "What Won't Junk Removal Companies Take";
Junk Professionals "What We Take / Don't Take"; dumpster accepted-items guides.

## Refrigerant rule (EPA Section 608)

Clean Air Act Section 608 (40 CFR Part 82, Subpart F): knowingly venting
refrigerant during service or disposal is prohibited; refrigerant must be
recovered with certified equipment, and technicians who service or dispose of
refrigerant-bearing appliances must hold EPA Section 608 certification.
Published framing for this estate: refrigerant appliances are hauled and routed
so the refrigerant is recovered by certified technicians before the appliance
is processed. The company claims NO refrigerant-recovery certification.
Sources: epa.gov Section 608 recordkeeping page; Contracting Business EPA 608
certification summary.

## Process chain (industry practice, adopted as the service design)

Survey (items inventoried where they sit; access noted) → quote → scheduling →
removal (lift / carry / load from where items sit; protect floors, walls,
doorways; standard pieces taken apart when a piece must come apart to leave
safely) → sorting after pickup (first pass: usable goods toward donation;
second pass: recyclables — scrap metal, cardboard, clean wood, e-waste; third
pass: regulated-stream items toward their facilities) → lawful disposal of the
remainder (transfer station / landfill; C&D debris to facilities that accept
it). Sorting happens before compaction; donation takes furniture, working
appliances, tools, household goods usable as-is; scrap yards grade ferrous and
non-ferrous metal separately.
Sources: Polk Services "What Happens to Your Junk After It's Hauled Away";
Tech Primex post-pickup sorting; Business Outstanding post-removal process.

## Pricing — domain education only (never company prices)

The industry prices mostly by truck volume in fractions — minimum pickup,
quarter, half, three-quarter, full — with weight-based (per ton) pricing as the
alternative model, per-item flat rates for single pickups, and hourly pricing
from some local haulers. Cost drivers: volume, density / weight (landfills bill
haulers by weight — tipping fees), labor and access (stairs, long carries),
distance, disposal fees. Published 2026 U.S. guide ranges: minimum pickup
roughly $70–$175; quarter load $150–$300; half load $250–$550; full truckload
$550–$1,000; a standard junk truck holds roughly 15–18 cubic yards; hourly
haulers $50–$150/hr. These figures appear ONLY inside the labeled industry-
background material and D5 with sources named. The company's model is Henry's
stated one: the full scope of each job is reviewed — scope, distance,
manpower, equipment — and priced at one flat rate in writing before work
begins, never hourly.
Sources: WastePlace junk-removal cost guide; Housecall Pro junk removal
pricing guide 2026; MyQuoteIQ 2026 cost calculator ranges; AnythingNeat
truckload / weight pricing explainer; DropCurb hourly-vs-flat explainer.

## Cleanout types and triggers (personas)

Estate cleanouts (executors, families), eviction and foreclosure cleanouts
(landlords, property managers), pre-listing clears (realtors, sellers),
downsizing, renovation debris (homeowners, contractors), office cleanouts and
fixture removal (businesses), garage / basement / attic accumulations,
appliance-replacement haul-away, hot tub removal (tub drained and disconnected
from power and water before the shell is cut and hauled — the disconnect is a
pre-pickup condition), storm and seasonal yard debris.
