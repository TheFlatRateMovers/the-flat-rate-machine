## Storage, explained — industry background, not our terms

Everything in this section is general industry information from mover and regulator sources. It explains how storage works in general. It is NOT The Flat Rate Movers' storage terms: our storage terms — leased storage, any item type, 30-day minimum stay, customer access while stored, lease terms vary, and storage quoted per job, monthly, or by volume — are stated in the sections above.

**Storage types (industry).** Short-term storage covers days to weeks — closing gaps, overlapping leases. Long-term storage covers months to years — renovations, deployments, downsizing, estate transitions, staging. Containerized or vaulted storage packs belongings into sealed wooden vaults at the home, inventoried and stored intact, so they are handled less; one vault holds about one room, and one published vault size is 7.5' × 7' × 5', about 245 cubic feet. Storage-in-transit (SIT) is temporary warehouse storage during a move. Other industry forms include vehicle, boat, and RV storage; commercial storage for inventory, files, fixtures, FF&E, and records with chain-of-custody, plus receiving, staging, retail overflow, and seasonal inventory; fine art, antique, and high-value storage; and temperature- or climate-controlled storage.

**Storage-in-transit rules (industry, FMCSA / NCUC).** SIT is federally defined temporary warehouse storage during a move, capped at 180 days; after 180 days it converts to permanent storage under a separate warehouse contract, with written notice required covering the conversion date, a 9-month claim window, the end of transit liability, and the warehouseman's rules and charges. Under SIT, goods stay under the bill of lading and the valuation chosen for the move — Released Value at $0.60 per pound, with Full Value Protection available; after conversion, coverage shifts to warehouse-receipt terms. SIT rates are typically weight-based rather than per-container, billed in 30-day increments, sometimes prorated. One SIT rule matters for leased space: NCUC states SIT shipments must be held in a warehouse owned by the carrier or a public warehouse in which the carrier has a long-term lease agreement. We describe our storage as leased storage.

**Inventory and protection (industry practice).** Numbered tags with condition noted at load-in; furniture blanket-wrapped; mirrors and art boxed; mattresses and upholstery covered; goods palletized or vaulted; held in secured, access-controlled space; climate control for wood, electronics, and sensitive items. The same inventory governs release, with a tag check at redelivery.

**Access models (industry).** Business-hours access; advance notice of 24 hours or 48 hours in published examples; attendant-assisted access, sometimes at an hourly rate; partial retrieval of selected items, sometimes for a small handling fee. None of these is our rule — they are industry models, listed so the options are understood.

**Pricing models (industry figures only — never our prices).** Published figures from other movers, listed for education: SIT at $0.40–$0.75 per cubic foot per month plus about $75 per vault in each direction (one Florida source); vault storage at $85–$115 per vault per month (one California source); $85 per vault per month plus a $100 setup fee (one Massachusetts source); monthly pricing by home size from one New Jersey mover (studio $130–$250, 2-bedroom $250–$400, 3–4 bedroom $400–$650); self-storage 5×10 units at $60–$220 depending on market and climate control. Pricing drivers in the industry are volume, weight, or cubic footage; climate control; duration; and location. Month-to-month billing and multi-month discounts are individual movers' offers, not industry rules. No figure in this paragraph is The Flat Rate Movers' price.

**Typical exclusions (industry education only — not our list).** Other movers commonly exclude perishables, live plants and animals, flammables, fuel and chemicals, illegal substances, unlicensed firearms, and hazardous materials. That is industry practice. Our published scope statement is "any type of items" with the examples above.

**Climate rationale (industry).** Stable temperature and humidity protect wood furniture, electronics, books, photographs, and fabrics; one premium published example is 18–24°C with 40–60% humidity. This paragraph states industry rationale only.
