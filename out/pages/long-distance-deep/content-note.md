# Content note — Long-Distance Moving deep-data package

## What the existing pages already say

- **The thesis, everywhere:** flat rate in writing before the truck rolls; hourly pricing on a long-distance move is "a blank cheque" (hub). Every one of the 12 scanned LD divs says "flat rate" 7–14 times. This is the category's spine and this package does not touch it.
- **The dispatch story:** Winchester junction where I-81 meets I-66, corridor network I-81/I-66/I-70 from both branches, Martinsburg Eastern Panhandle origin. The corridor pages carry the audience-as-coverage-pool disclaimer (areas are the audience, not town-on-highway claims).
- **The scope language (satellites, not the hub):** "moving across state lines" (interstate Winchester), "cross-country moves quoted flat" (cross-country page), five Virginia-to-* destination lanes, Miami, Las Vegas (gun-safe cross-ref). The hub div itself contains none of this language — it is careful where the satellites are bold. That asymmetry is recorded, not smoothed over: see the authorization gate below.
- **The branch asymmetry:** Martinsburg's LD page (992 words, FAQ) is nearly twice the Winchester hub (531 words, FAQ). The Winchester guide (433) and interstate guide (442) add researcher depth but no buyer-concern depth.
- **What the pages never say (verified by scan, 12 divs):** delivery window, delivery spread, valuation, released value, full value, tracking, storage in transit, bill of lading, binding. The category's pages answer "what does it cost" (flat) and "where from" (corridors) but never "when will it arrive", "what is it covered for", or "what kind of estimate is it".

## What this package adds

- **A full node where three broken ones stood:** thin head node, Martinsburg-skewed entity fragment, absent best-graph fragment → one full two-branch node (both graphs + head upgrade, same @id, casing unified).
- **D1–D8 full datasets** (research doc §6): operations, distance/shipment bands, route/corridor/destination network, delivery-window models (domain only), commercial terms (domain estimate types + valuation + cost drivers; flat-rate-in-writing as the ONLY company term), personas/triggers, protection/inventory/compliance (including the authorization gate as a verification standard), areas/dispatch/quote path.
- **An honest authorization record:** what the pages say about interstate/cross-country scope, quoted and sourced, next to the unconfirmed authorization status — with the wording-review consequence stated for a "no authority" answer. The package neither endorses the satellite language nor rewrites it; that decision belongs to the owner after Q1.
- **A storage cross-reference with its gate intact:** storage attested (offered, leased, any item types); SIT terms and SIT qualification explicitly NOT claimed, with the domain 180-day rule and the carrier-owned/long-term-leased warehouse test stated as domain facts the owner's leased storage has not been tested against.

## Page-mechanism decision: NO new page — with evidence

No content.md / meta.json / backlinks.md were staged, deliberately.

- The hub exists (531 words, FAQ, 1,984 impressions @ 49.4) and 16 more pages carry the service, including a 992-word branch page, a cross-country page, two interstate pages, a network page, two guides, five destination lanes, Miami, and two category indexes. The problem is position, vocabulary, and graph — not existence.
- A second long-distance hub would split the 1,984-impression hub and the 2,900-impression interstate page, the two largest pools in the category, and cannibalize the cross-country terms that already rank 4.0–7.1.
- The destination-lane question (five thin templated pages at 376–416 words) is a depth-or-consolidation question for Phase E on Q9 approval, not a new-page question now: the lanes exist, they are just thin.
- The missing intents (delivery window, valuation, estimate type) are education topics, not service pages: they become explainer follow-ups (§9) only after Phase H re-pulls show the queries arriving, or after Q2/Q3/Q4 attest company practice. Building them as pages now, on domain facts alone, would publish education dressed as service scope.

## Phase E depth brief (guidance only — live copy does not change in this package)

1. **Delivery-window block (hub + interstate pages, domain-labeled):** what a delivery spread is, why it exists (consolidation, route order, hours-of-service, weather), the 7–21 / 3–14 day published ranges, the 24–48h call-ahead pattern, and the essentials-kit preparation list. No company window, date, or tracking promise — the block teaches the concept and points at the quote form for the client's own dates.
2. **Valuation block (hub + guides, domain-labeled):** Full Value Protection vs Released Value ($0.60/lb, $15 for a 25-lb television), the $6/lb minimum declared value, the >$100/lb declaration rule, and the valuation-≠-insurance distinction. No company offering named until Q3.
3. **Estimate-type block (hub + guides, domain-labeled):** binding vs binding-not-to-exceed vs non-binding, the 110% delivery-day rule, balance within 30 days, and why the company's "flat rate in writing" answers the fear behind all three — without mapping it to an FMCSA type until Q4.
4. **Winchester hub parity:** expand the hub (531 words) toward Martinsburg depth (992 words) — placement step, inventory discipline, access planning — on Q9 approval.
5. **Destination-lane depth:** add one lane-specific paragraph each (what the lane entails: distance band, corridor routing in general terms, destination access considerations as planning inputs) or consolidate the five lanes into one lanes index — decision on Q9; never invent lane-specific service claims.
6. **Access vocabulary block:** stairs, elevator, long carry, shuttle, parking, building hours — as planning inputs that change quote scope, not as priced services, until Q7.
7. **Cross-links:** hub ↔ residential (placement, packing), ↔ packing (interstate pack guide), ↔ SIR/gun-safe (heavy pieces), ↔ storage (gated cross-ref only), ↔ military (cross-ref only), ↔ corridors (I-81/I-66/I-70).
