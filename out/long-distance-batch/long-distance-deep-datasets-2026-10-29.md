## 1. What long-distance moving IS (domain facts, researched + company facts, verified)

A long-distance move is a household or business relocation beyond the local area, priced and planned differently from a local move in every respect that matters to the buyer:

- **Distance threshold (domain).** Industry practice commonly treats moves beyond roughly 50 miles, or across a state line, as long-distance rather than local. The legal line that matters is the state line: a move across state lines is an interstate move governed by federal household-goods rules (49 CFR Part 375), while a move inside one state is intrastate even if it is long. This package uses "long-distance" for the distance concept and "interstate" only where a state line is actually crossed — the two words are not interchangeable, and the existing pages sometimes blur them (see §8 Q10).
- **Pricing basis (domain).** A long-distance move is priced from inventory weight and mileage, not by the hour. The carrier weighs the truck empty and loaded at a certified commercial scale; the difference is the shipment weight. Linehaul (the transport charge) is weight × distance-based rate, plus fuel surcharge, plus accessorial charges for everything beyond basic transport. That is why inventory accuracy matters more on a long-distance quote than on any other kind: every pound and every access condition changes the price.
- **Delivery model (domain).** Unlike a local move (same-day, exact time), a long-distance move delivers inside a delivery spread — a window of dates, typically 7–21 days for van-line shipments and 3–14 days in common published ranges, widening with distance and in peak season — because trucks consolidate shipments and deliver along a route. The driver normally calls 24–48 hours before arrival. An exact date generally requires an exclusive-use truck at a premium (domain). No delivery window, spread, or tracking promise is published by the company; all delivery-window content in this package is domain education until §8 Q2 is answered.
- **Paperwork model (domain).** The interstate move runs on written estimates (binding, binding-not-to-exceed, or non-binding — see §4/D5), an itemized inventory, and a bill of lading that is both contract and receipt. Valuation (Full Value Protection vs Released Value at $0.60/lb) is a liability level, not insurance. None of this paperwork is described on the company's long-distance pages today (0 mentions — see correction 3); the datasets below teach it as domain education, never as company procedure.
- **Company facts (verified, published).** Flat-rate long-distance moving from Winchester, VA and Martinsburg, WV; one price agreed in writing before the truck rolls ("Hourly pricing on a long-distance move is a blank cheque. We quote flat" — hub page); dispatched from the Winchester junction where I-81 meets I-66 and along the I-81/I-66/I-70 corridor network; SIR/KIM-GS handling for high-mass assets (1,500–3,500 lbs, floor deflection to L/360) as a conditional cross-reference for heavy pieces, not as the long-distance method itself.
