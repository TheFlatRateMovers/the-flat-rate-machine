## 1. What local and regional moving IS (domain facts, researched + company facts, verified)

- **Local moving (domain definition).** A local move is a relocation within the same city, metro area, or region, typically under 50–100 miles, usually completed in one day by one crew with one truck returning the same day. It is a pricing category, not a legal definition: the defining factors are service area, same-day completion, and local trucks/crews rather than long-haul equipment. Local (intrastate) moves are generally regulated by state rules, not the FMCSA's interstate rules. Sources: Extra Space local-vs-long-distance guide; Three Movers local-moving definition; Movers.com local-move costs; Grokipedia moving-company overview — all researched 2026-10-29, all labeled domain.
- **Regional moving (domain + company framing).** A regional move extends across a multi-county region — here, the Shenandoah Valley and the Eastern Panhandle, dispatched on I-81, I-66, and I-70 from both branches. The company publishes no mileage cutoff separating local from regional: its published framing is corridors + areas (hub: "Local and regional moves along the I-81, I-66 and I-70 corridors, from our two home bases in Winchester, VA and Martinsburg, WV"). The domain 50–100-mile band is context only; the company boundary question is an attestation item (§8 Q2), not a dataset claim.
- **Company facts (verified):** local and regional moves along the I-81, I-66, and I-70 corridors (services.json, attested 2026-10-28). The hub page's published scope: residential and commercial moves within the local and regional area; packing and unpacking; loading and unloading; high-mass pieces under SIR/KIM-GS conditionally (1,500–3,500 lbs, L/360 floor-deflection analysis); one flat price agreed in writing before move day — "No hourly clock, ever." Branch pages' published audience: homeowners, renters, families, and seniors. The hub FAQ settles base assignment: both bases dispatch across the region; Winchester serves the Shenandoah Valley side, Martinsburg the Eastern Panhandle side, service areas overlap at Moorefield, and "the quote form settles which crew and route fit your move" — that sentence is the company's published assignment rule, and this package repeats it rather than inventing its own.
- **What local/regional moving is NOT (boundaries, stated so the datasets do not overclaim):** it is not long-distance moving (separate category, separate node, cross-referenced only); it is not residential moving as such (separate category — the hub covers homes AND businesses, but the residential node owns the home-move depth; cross-referenced, never duplicated); it is not office moving, senior moving, or any specialty category (all separate nodes, cross-referenced only); it is not a same-day/next-day availability promise (gated — §8 Q1); it is not a mileage-band promise (gated — §8 Q2); it is not a small-job minimum or studio-move product (gated — §8 Q3).

## 3. How it is done (the process chain, researched + matched to published company steps)

Domain process (researched 2026-10-29, from mover process guides — stated as industry practice, not company claims):

1. **Survey and written quote** — an on-site or virtual survey lists goods, packing needs, and access (stairs, elevators, parking, doorways); the mover returns a written quote stating what is included. The company's published version: "Get My Flat Rate Quote" (Short Quick Quote default / Detailed Project toggle, preserving From, To, and What's moving) plus the film-only video-walkthrough survey room (human-made estimate; film only — never lift, move, open, or climb anything heavy or locked). The hub FAQ adds the assignment rule: the quote form settles which base's crew and route fit the move.
2. **Packing, room by room (or client-packed)** — crew packs room by room, labeling every box by room; fragile items wrapped individually; furniture blanketed and stretch-wrapped. Company-published options (via the hub's "packing and unpacking" scope and the residential page's packing options, cross-referenced): full, partial, or fragile-only packing, plus unpacking at the other end; client may pack themselves and the company loads, moves, and places.
3. **Preparation and protection** — floor runners and doorway protection; furniture disassembly where needed (beds, wardrobes, shelving; legs/leaves removed to clear tight turns). Company-published (hub + residential cross-ref): loading and unloading with furniture protection; disassembly/reassembly of standard furniture, planned up front (residential page scope, applied inside local moves).
4. **Loading, sequenced** — heavy items low and first, boxes staged by room, fragile secured on top, everything strapped against movement. Same crew loads, drives, and unloads on short-distance moves (domain pattern).
5. **Short transport** — closed truck on the planned route along I-81, I-66, or I-70 as the corridor fits; local moves typically same-day (domain pattern — the company's same-day availability is NOT claimed, §8 Q1). Areas are the coverage pool, never a claim that a listed town sits on a particular highway (corridor-page disclaimer rule, preserved here).
6. **Unloading and placement, room by room** — cartons to their labeled rooms, furniture placed and reassembled. Company-published: unloading + careful placement (hub scope; residential page's placement language, cross-referenced).
7. **Walkthrough and close-out** — final walkthrough against the inventory; debris cleared where included. Debris scope for local moves is an attestation question (§8 Q5), not a page claim.

Access sequence inside the chain (domain facts, apartment and house alike): confirm doorways, elevators, and staircases fit the largest pieces before moving day; reserve freight/service elevators and loading zones where buildings require it; match crew arrival to the building's moving window; protect common areas (rails, floors, hallways) on the path. These are planning inputs to the quote, not company capability claims. The cost drivers that move a local quote in the market — stairs, elevators, long-carry distance, parking access, packing level, heavy items — are domain facts (see D5); the company's quote captures From, To, and What's moving, and the survey room films access conditions.
