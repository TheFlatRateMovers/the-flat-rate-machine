## 1. Floor deflection L/360 — the standard behind the SIR floor check

Published company copy states a floor-deflection analysis to L/360 before a heavy asset
moves, with load spreading. L/360 is the standard residential floor deflection limit in
the building codes: a floor joist must not deflect more than its span divided by 360
under live load (for an 18-foot span, 0.6 inches). Sources consulted 2026-10-29:

- https://engineerfix.com/what-are-the-code-requirements-for-subfloors/ — "This is
  quantified by the standard L/360 criteria, where 'L' is the joist span length. A floor
  system must not deflect more than 1/360th of its total span length"; residential living
  areas 40 pounds per square foot live load.
- https://www.houzz.com/magazine/building-or-remodeling-get-the-lowdown-on-load-codes-stsetivw-vs~21781595
  — "The code requires that this deflection be limited to L/360, where L is the length of
  the unsupported span."

Use in staged copy: the L/360 check is stated as the method ("floor-deflection check to
L/360 before anything lifts"), never as a company engineering credential. The company
method name (SIR Structural Integrity Relocation, KIM-GS Protocol, 1,500–3,500 lb band)
remains company vocabulary from its own files; these sources only confirm that L/360 is
a real, standard deflection criterion, which is why the copy does not need to hedge it.

## 2. ISPM 15 — the standard behind export packing

Published company copy states ISPM 15 export packing and custom crates. ISPM 15 is
"Regulation of Wood Packaging Material in International Trade", a phytosanitary standard
of the International Plant Protection Convention (IPPC): wood packaging material must be
treated (heat treatment to a core temperature of at least 56 °C for at least 30 minutes,
or fumigation / dielectric heating) and permanently marked with the IPPC symbol, country
code, producer code, and treatment code. Sources consulted 2026-10-29:

- https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-agriculture-food-and-the-marine/publications/wood-packaging-material-and-ispm-15/
  — ISPM 15 definition, treatment and marking requirements, exemptions (wood 6 mm or
  less; processed wood).
- https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/40409ef?reqfrom=share
  — U.S. Customs and Border Protection trade notice on ISPM 15 marking enforcement
  (IPPC logo, ISO country code, treatment facility number, hyphen separation).

Use in staged copy: "ISPM 15 export packing" is stated as the service, with the full
expansion "International Standards for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15" where the page
already gives it. No treatment facility, registration, or certification is claimed for
the company, because none is in the verified files.

## 3. Valuation vocabulary — domain education, never company terms

The datasets already label valuation as domain education: Full Value Protection (mover
liable for replacement value of lost or damaged items) versus Released Value Protection
(no extra cost, liability limited to 60 cents per pound per article). Federal consumer
guidance confirms the same two options and the same 60-cent figure. Source consulted
2026-10-29:

- https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/faq?keyword=&term_node_tid_depth=23066 — "There are two
  types of moving company liability coverage interstate movers must offer their
  customers: full value protection or released value protection. With full value
  protection, your mover is liable for the total replacement value of any lost or
  damaged items in your shipment. With released value protection, there's no
  additional cost but you'll only receive up to 60 cents per pound for an item."

Use in staged copy: none. This appendix records the source only so a future editor does
not re-introduce valuation as a company offering. The staged visibility rewrites claim
no valuation offering; the company's published commercial term remains one flat rate
quoted in writing before move day, under "Guaranteed Flat Rate or Your Move is Free!".
