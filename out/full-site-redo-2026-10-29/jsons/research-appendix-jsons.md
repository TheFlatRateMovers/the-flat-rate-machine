## 1. Estimates, valuation, and price certainty (residential + commercial datasets)

- FMCSA household-goods framework, 49 CFR Part 375, as summarized in
  consumer guidance: a binding estimate is the most the mover can require
  at delivery for listed services; a non-binding estimate is weight-based,
  with no more than 110% collectible at delivery and the balance billed
  after 30 days; an interstate estimate rests on a physical (in-person or
  virtual) survey unless waived in writing.
  - https://www.freightwaves.com/checkpoint/types-moving-companies/
  - https://box-track.com/free/questions-to-ask-movers
- Valuation: Full Value Protection (repair, replace, or pay current
  replacement value; minimum $6 per pound) versus Released Value (60 cents
  per pound per article, at no charge, chosen in writing); valuation is the
  mover's liability, not third-party insurance.
  - https://www.freightwaves.com/checkpoint/types-moving-companies/
  - https://www.bigbox.com/blog/cost-of-moving-from-san-diego-to-another-state/
- Why this matters for the rewrites: the datasets' domain-education passages
  on binding vs. non-binding vs. not-to-exceed estimates and on Full Value
  vs. Released Value are standard industry education. The rewrites keep
  that education, labelled as domain education, and delete only the
  hedging framing that withheld the topic — they do NOT add a company coverage
  offering, price, deposit, discount, or fee that was not already published.

## 2. Commercial relocation: phased moves and downtime-first planning

- Industry relocation guides converge on: plan 8–12 weeks out for most
  business relocations (3–6 months for larger offices; more lead time in
  peak season May–September); phase by department or floor; move
  non-customer-facing teams midweek and customer-facing teams over a
  weekend; pre-stage and label by destination zone; sequence IT
  last-off/first-on; book elevators, loading docks, certificates of
  insurance, and parking permits at both ends with buffer time.
  - https://www.americanvanlines.com/complete-commercial-relocation-guide-for-moving-midsize-offices-of-50-200-employees/
  - https://www.americanvanlines.com/top-8-commercial-relocation-tips-for-business-continuity-and-seamless-transition/
  - https://safeshipmoving.com/services/corporate-moving-services/
- Why this matters: the commercial dataset's operation chain (survey,
  quote, phased plan, pack and label, sequenced move, install and place,
  verify, close-out; what moves first, what moves last, what works on day
  one) and its building-input list (freight-elevator reservation, dock
  access, move hours, advance notice, security protocol, parking/permits)
  are standard commercial practice. The rewrites state that chain
  confidently and state that building requirements are confirmed as quote
  inputs — they do NOT claim a dedicated coordinator, after-hours offering,
  or building-paperwork issuance that was not already published.

## 3. Heavy assets, floors, and safe moving (SIR / gun-safe dataset)

- International Residential Code (IRC) residential floors: living areas
  designed for a 40 pounds-per-square-foot live load (sleeping rooms 30
  psf), with deflection limited to L/360 — span length divided by 360
  (e.g., a 10-foot span deflects no more than 1/3 inch).
  - https://engineerfix.com/what-are-the-code-requirements-for-subfloors/
  - https://www.hunker.com/13400811/how-to-calculate-floor-load-capacity/
- Safe-moving industry practice: the safe is emptied completely before
  move day (firearms, ammunition, documents, valuables out and transported
  separately); stairwell dimensions and weight-bearing capacity are
  assessed during quoting; stair-climbing dollies and controlled descent
  are used for basement stairs; plywood/hardboard runners and floor
  protection distribute concentrated weight; doorframe clearances are
  measured before lifting; the safe is secured upright in the truck and
  placed exactly where the owner specifies, with re-bolting arranged
  separately by the owner where required.
  - https://starmovers.com/services/gun-safe-moving/
  - https://www.safeboundmoving.com/services/safe-movers
  - https://serenitymovingservices.com/oklahoma/gun-safe/
- Why this matters: the gun-safe rewrites keep the published SIR band
  (1,500–3,500 lbs), L/360 check, load spreading, floor protection, and
  verified placement, and replace hedging framing that withheld topics
  with the published survey process (make/model/weight, addresses, access
  at both ends). Safes outside the band, stairs, emptying preparation,
  re-anchoring, vault scope, calibration-sensitive handling, and
  long-distance scope are framed as survey topics — NOT as newly claimed
  company offerings. No engineering certification is claimed.

## 4. ISPM 15 export packing

- ISPM 15 (International Standards for Phytosanitary Measures No. 15),
  "Regulation of Wood Packaging Material in International Trade," from
  the IPPC (FAO): wood packaging material over 6 mm thick must be
  debarked, treated (heat treatment to a core temperature of at least
  56 °C for at least 30 minutes, or approved fumigation/dielectric
  heating), and marked with the official IPPC mark (country code,
  producer code, treatment code). Processed-wood products (plywood, OSB,
  veneer) and wood 6 mm or thinner are exempt.
  - https://www.gov.pl/web/piorin-en/wood-packaging
  - https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-agriculture-food-and-the-marine/publications/wood-packaging-material-and-ispm-15/
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISPM_15
- Why this matters: the ISPM 15 per-page rewrite expands the one-line
  live description with exactly this standard treatment/marking framing.
  It does not claim a treatment facility, license, or certification for
  the company beyond the published export-packing service.

## 5. Household, senior, piano/antique, and warehouse practice

- Household operation sequence used across the rewrites (survey and
  written quote; room-by-room packing at full/partial/fragile-only levels;
  sequenced loading with heavy items low and first and fragile secured on
  top; transport on the planned route; room-by-room unloading, placement,
  and standard-furniture reassembly planned up front; closing walkthrough)
  is standard full-service moving practice, already published in the
  residential dataset; the rewrites carry it into the thin per-page
  descriptions without adding prices or claims.
- White-glove handling (blanket/stretch wrap, padded transport, custom
  crates for pieces that need more than padding) and piano/antique care
  as single planned pieces with surveyed route/clearance and verified
  placement are standard specialty-moving practice; the rewrites use that
  framing for the piano-antique file and keep custom crating as the
  cross-reference for pieces that need more than padding.
- Warehouse/transload practice used in the rewrites (containers unloaded,

  Inland Port and Port of Virginia chain) is already published in the
  transloading and warehouse files; the rewrites expand the one-line
  descriptions with that same chain, nothing new.
