## 1. What moving-and-storage IS (domain facts, researched + company facts, verified)

- **Moving-and-storage as a combined service (domain definition)** = a household or commercial move with a storage interval between move-out and move-in: the mover packs and loads, holds the shipment, then redelivers and places it when the destination is ready. It is one coordinated plan with two move days and a hold in between — not two separate hires (a move plus a self-storage rental). The storage interval is the product: it exists because the two dates do not line up.
- **Storage-in-transit (SIT) vs long-term/permanent storage (domain distinction).** SIT is temporary storage provided by the mover between pickup and delivery, meant to bridge a gap of days or a few weeks — a delayed closing, an overlapping lease, a renovation finishing late. Under standard moving tariffs, SIT is commonly capped (a widely cited limit is 180 days); past that limit the shipment typically converts to permanent storage under a separate warehouse contract, with terms re-agreed before the conversion rather than sprung on the customer. Long-term storage is open-ended by design: downsizing transitions, extended temporary housing, seasonal holds, staged moves where final placement decisions take months. The two are different contracts with different access and billing logic — this package never merges them.
- **Vaulted warehouse storage vs self-storage vs portable containers (domain taxonomy, §4).** Vaulted warehouse storage: the mover pad-wraps belongings, seals them in wooden vaults (a commonly cited size is 7′ × 5′ × 8′), and holds them in a secured, managed warehouse; access is by appointment with advance notice (commonly 24–48 hours) so staff can pull specific vaults; partial retrieval of selected vaults is possible without closing the whole account. Self-storage: a unit the customer rents, locks, and manages; frequent self-access, but the customer does all handling and the gap move is handled four times (load truck, unload into unit, load from unit, unload at home) versus twice with mover storage (one professional load-in, one redelivery). Portable containers: a container dropped at the driveway, loaded by the customer at their own pace, hauled away and stored — convenient for slow decluttering, but subject to HOA/driveway time limits (commonly 24–48 hours) and to tilt-and-shift during lifting.
- **Inventory and condition (domain practice).** Mover storage runs on an itemized inventory: every vault is assigned to one client, tagged, and matched to a household inventory list; furniture stays blanket-wrapped for the whole stay; condition is recorded at intake and checked at release. Barcoded vault tracking is the industry pattern for keeping shipments from mixing. These are domain practices, not company claims — the company's published inventory practice is the quote form (From, To, What's moving) and the film-only survey room.
- **Company facts (verified, move side only):** flat-rate house and apartment moving, local, regional and long-distance (services.json); loading, transport and unloading; furniture protection and careful placement; disassembly/reassembly of standard furniture planned up front; full/partial/fragile-only packing plus unpacking; white-glove care for antiques and high-value pieces; custom crates; heavy pieces under SIR/KIM-GS conditionally; one price agreed in writing before move day; "Get My Flat Rate Quote" with Short (Quick Quote) default / Detailed Project toggle preserving From, To, and What's moving; film-only video-walkthrough survey room with a human-made estimate. NO company storage fact exists to list — that absence is the gate, stated here rather than papered over.
- **What moving-and-storage is NOT (boundaries, stated so the datasets do not overclaim):** it is not warehouse storage for freight (the industrial-commercial warehouse floor and transload staging are a different category — goods staged for onward shipment, not households held between homes); it is not self-storage rental (the company publishes no unit rental); it is not PODS/container rental as such (PODS handling exists as an industrial serviceType and GBP product, but no PODS-based household storage service is published); it is not a claim that the company holds goods indefinitely, offers any access window, or charges any storage rate.

## 2. Who needs it (buyer personas, researched + GSC evidence)

- **Closing-gap movers (the core persona)** — the sale closes on one date, the purchase on another, and the household has nowhere to land in between. GSC: "moving and storage" 85 impressions at 1.0 is this persona's query in its purest form. Domain guidance: booking roughly 4–6 weeks before move-out day is the safe target for a gap move, since one crew handling both the move and the hold absorbs a shifted closing date better than a last-minute scramble; a short extension may cost nothing extra while a longer one can add a billing period — terms in writing before booking are the protection.
- **Renovators** — floors, paint, or a build-out finishing after move-out; fresh work is far easier without a house full of boxes in the way. The hold ends when the work is done, which is why redelivery scheduling (not a fixed date) is the defining feature of this persona's plan.
- **Downsizers and estate transitions** — a larger home sold, a smaller place (or a decision about what fits) not yet settled; the hold covers the overflow while the household sorts what fits. Cross-reference only to the senior category (downsizing transitions) and the estate-relocation page (under the packing service) — never duplicated here.
- **Staged movers** — a household moved in phases: part now, part later, part never (donated/sold). Partial retrieval (selected vaults back before the hold ends) is the domain feature this persona needs; it is gated here as a company capability (§8).
- **Seasonal and temporary-housing holds** — travel, temporary postings, and extended stays where the household's goods wait months rather than weeks. This is the long-term side of the taxonomy (§4); whether the company serves it at all is §8 Q1, not assumed from the SIT pattern.
- **Corporate staging and fit-out gaps** — offices and commercial spaces between leases or mid-build-out, where furniture, fixtures, and files wait for the space to be ready. Cross-reference only to office/industrial-offices categories; no commercial storage claim is made here.
- **Long-distance delivery-window movers** — on longer routes movers deliver within a window rather than a fixed day, so goods may wait on either end. Cross-reference only to the long-distance category.
- **The actual searcher (domain pattern):** a person whose two dates just stopped lining up, comparing a self-storage unit against a mover-held solution on three questions — how many times will my things be handled, can I get at them if I need them, and what does the gap actually cost. The honest domain answer (handling count 4 vs 2; access anytime vs by appointment; one bill vs separate bills) is the education this category's datasets carry — labeled domain, never presented as the company's terms.

## 3. How it is done (the process chain, researched + matched to published company steps)

Domain process (researched 2026-10-29, from multiple mover storage guides — stated as industry practice, not company claims). Company-published steps are marked [COMPANY]; everything else is domain practice for the storage interval.

1. **Survey and written quote [COMPANY pattern + domain extension]** — an in-person or video survey lists goods, packing needs, access, and the expected hold length; the mover returns a written estimate stating what is included. Company-published version: "Get My Flat Rate Quote" (Short/Detailed toggle) plus the film-only video-walkthrough survey room (human-made estimate; film only — never lift, move, open, or climb anything heavy or locked). Domain extension for gap moves: the survey should capture the expected hold length, because volume + duration + handling on each end are the three visible cost drivers of a storage estimate (domain pricing logic, §6/D5).
2. **Packing, room by room [COMPANY]** — crew packs room by room, labeling every box by room; fragile items wrapped individually; furniture blanketed and stretch-wrapped; specialty cartons for dishes, mirrors, art, electronics; mattress bags; wardrobe cartons. Company-published options: full, partial, or fragile-only packing, plus unpacking at the other end; client may pack themselves and the company loads, moves, and places.
3. **Loading, sequenced [COMPANY]** — heavy items low and first, boxes staged by room, fragile secured on top, everything strapped against movement. Company-published: loading, transport and unloading; blanket-wrapped end to end (style-example standard, honestly captioned).
4. **Intake inventory and condition [DOMAIN]** — goods listed vault by vault against an itemized inventory; furniture stays blanket-wrapped; condition recorded at intake. Barcoded or tagged vault tracking keeps one client's shipment from mixing with another's. This step is what makes redelivery verifiable — and it is precisely the step no company publication describes for storage, which is why it is domain-labeled here and gated as a capability (§8).
5. **Vaulting and hold [DOMAIN]** — pad-wrapped belongings sealed in wooden vaults in a secured, managed warehouse (commonly cited vault size 7′ × 5′ × 8′). The hold runs for the agreed interval: days to weeks for SIT, longer for permanent storage under its own contract. Access during the hold is by appointment with advance notice (commonly 24–48 hours) so staff can pull specific vaults to a viewing bay; partial retrieval of selected vaults is possible without closing the whole account. NONE of this paragraph is a company claim — facility, vaulting, access window, and partial-retrieval capability are all §8 questions.
6. **Redelivery scheduling [DOMAIN]** — when the destination is ready, redelivery is scheduled (the destination-ready trigger, not a fixed date, is the planning variable for gap moves). Redelivery distance from the warehouse to the new home is one of the visible cost drivers (domain). Company-published parallel: final-mile delivery exists as its own service for last-leg delivery — cross-reference only; redelivery from a storage hold is not claimed as a final-mile service here.
7. **Unloading and placement, room by room [COMPANY]** — cartons to their labeled rooms, furniture placed and reassembled, beds/wardrobes rebuilt. Company-published: careful placement; unpacking available.
8. **Walkthrough and close-out [COMPANY pattern + DOMAIN extension]** — final walkthrough against the inventory; release condition checked against intake condition (domain practice — the mirror of step 4). Debris scope after unpacking is an attestation question in the residential package (§8 Q5 there); it is not claimed here either.

Access and handling rules inside the chain (domain facts): confirm doorways, elevators, and staircases fit the largest pieces before move day; reserve freight/service elevators and loading zones where buildings require it; for the storage leg, confirm truck access at the warehouse (aisle width, overhead clearance for vault handling) and the facility's gate/office hours. Mover valuation coverage commonly ends once items are placed in a third-party unit (domain guidance: check homeowners/renters policy or facility coverage) — which is exactly why mover-held storage and self-storage are different risk shapes, and why valuation during a company hold is §8 Q8, not a dataset field.
