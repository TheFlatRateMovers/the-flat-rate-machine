## §1 — What senior moving is

**Published company scope (live senior page, verified 2026-10-29).** Senior moving from The Flat Rate Movers is a flat-rate residential move for seniors downsizing, transitioning to assisted or independent living, or relocating closer to family, across the Shenandoah Valley and Eastern Panhandle. What the page actually publishes, and nothing more:

- Packing support for senior moves, including fragile items and keepsakes.
- The free video-walkthrough survey room: film only — never lift, move, open, or climb anything heavy or locked — with a human-made estimate. The survey room's Senior category covers main living areas, bedroom and personal rooms, and keepsakes and fragile items, one prompt per screen. Media auto-deletes 24 hours after upload.
- One flat price agreed in writing before move day. No hourly clock, ever. Tagline: "Guaranteed Flat Rate or Your Move is Free!"
- If a senior move involves a very heavy item — a high-mass asset in the 1,500–3,500 lb range — that item moves under Structural Integrity Relocation (SIR), with floor deflection analysis to L/360, as part of the same flat-rate move. SIR is NOT claimed for senior moves generally; it applies only where such an asset is actually involved.
- The FAQ states it directly: no price, discount, or special offer is stated for senior moves. Every move is quoted flat per job, agreed in writing before move day.
- The page lists the service as "Senior Advocacy Logistics" (alternateName). That is a listing name on the page. No advocacy, care-coordination, facility-liaison, or case-management service is described anywhere on the page, and none is claimed in this package. Advocacy scope beyond the name is gated (§8 Q2/Q7).
- One two-branch page, not branch pages. The GSC query is generic ("senior moving"), so the category is served by one page carrying both branches distinctly — never blended names, hours, areas, or ratings.

**Domain context (labeled domain, web research 2026-10-29 — NOT company scope).** Across the senior-moving market, a senior move is distinguished from a standard residential move by pace, emotion, and coordination load, not by the truck: decisions about decades of belongings take time; the pace is intentionally slower than a standard move; families often coordinate from out of town; and the destination is often smaller than the origin, so what fits must be decided before move day. The wider market splits the work into two roles that this package keeps strictly apart: the MOVER (packs, carries, transports, places) and the SENIOR MOVE MANAGER (plans, sorts, coordinates, settles — and typically does NOT drive the truck or carry furniture). The National Association of Senior Move Managers (NASMM) defines senior move management as "the profession that assists older adults, individuals, and families with downsizing, relocation or simplifying their homes"; NASMM members agree to a code of ethics and carry liability insurance, and the association maintains a searchable directory. The Flat Rate Movers publishes NO NASMM membership and NO move-manager service. Move-manager services appear in this document as domain context only, so the page never borrows them by implication. What competitors commonly publish for senior moves — and what this package does NOT claim for this company — includes: patient, unhurried crews; furniture disassembly and reassembly; floor protection; coordination with facility staff on scheduling and access; downsizing and sorting support; donation coordination and receipts; estate-sale coordination; unpacking and furniture placement so the senior arrives to a livable home; and space/floor planning for the new residence. Each of those is a gated question in §8, not a company fact.

**What senior moving is NOT (published boundaries).** Not a care service: no medical, health-care, personal-care, or caregiving claim appears on the page or in this package. Not a discount program: no senior price, discount, or special offer is stated. Not a facility service: no assisted-living facility, community, or partner is named. Not a storage service: storage needs cross-reference the moving-and-storage category (attested: leased storage, any item types, 30-day minimum, customer access yes, leases vary, SIT qualification not claimed, pricing basis per job, monthly, or by volume; no rates attested) and are never claimed inside the senior datasets beyond that cross-reference. Not a general SIR claim: SIR applies only to senior moves that actually involve a 1,500–3,500 lb asset.

## §4 — Taxonomy

**Transition types (published + domain, labeled).** Published (live page): downsizing; transitioning to assisted living; transitioning to independent living; relocating closer to family. Domain (search motivation + market practice, never company scope): retirement-community moves; 55+ community moves; estate-settlement moves; health-change-driven moves (a health change as the REASON for the move — motivation only, never a care claim); temporary-to-permanent transitions (e.g., a stay that becomes a move — domain pattern only).

**Dwelling transitions (domain framing of published scope).** Origin: a family home occupied for years or decades (domain: the sorting burden scales with years in the home). Destination: a smaller home, a 55+ community unit, an independent-living unit, or an assisted-living unit (published destination classes: assisted living, independent living, smaller home implied by "downsizing", family-proximate home implied by "closer to family"). The destination being smaller than the origin is the structural reason downsizing decisions dominate senior moves (domain). No destination facility, community, or unit size is published for this company; no floor-plan or fit analysis is published as a company service.

**Destination and building classes (planning inputs only).** Senior living communities, 55+ communities, apartment-style units, single-family downsizes (domain classes). Building inputs that shape any senior move's planning — elevator booking, parking, move-in windows, dock access, hallway protection — are DOMAIN planning inputs (competitor pages publish facility coordination; this company does not). They appear in D7 as inputs, never as company capabilities, until §8 Q2 is answered.

**Item classes (published + domain).** Published: fragile items and keepsakes (packing support); high-mass assets 1,500–3,500 lbs (SIR conditional only). Domain: furniture (including heirlooms, antiques, china collections, artwork — individual wrapping and gentle handling in market practice); clothing and personal effects; books, photographs, and documents; kitchen and household goods; medical-adjacent durable items (NOT a company class — no medical moving claim is made for senior moves; medical moving is a separate category and is cross-referenced only). Keepsakes are the highest-trust class in the category: irreplaceable, sentimental, and the class where handling care is judged (domain + live page emphasis).

**Move-size bands (domain only).** The market describes senior moves by rooms downsized (a room or two to a full family home) and by destination size (a suite vs a small home). No company size band, minimum, or crew rule is published for senior moves, and none is stated here. Size bands in D2/D3 are domain context for the datasets' volume fields, never company scope.

**Audience vs service-name note.** "Senior Advocacy Logistics" is the page's alternateName. It is NOT a second service, NOT a care program, and NOT a credential. The datasets carry it as alternateName with a scope note: name only; advocacy services (liaison, coordination, case management) are gated, not claimed. If the owner later attests an advocacy scope, it joins the datasets as a new attested fact — it is not backfilled from the name.
